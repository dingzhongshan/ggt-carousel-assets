Flat in the morning. Tight and full by bedtime. Sound familiar?

You're not imagining the bloat, and you're not alone. Research suggests 16% to 31% of people deal with bloating. Some gas is normal too, especially during or after meals.

Here's my take: stop hunting for one secret "bad" food. Gas comes from two places. Air you swallow, and carbs your gut bacteria break down in your large intestine. Eating fast, gum, fizzy drinks and big meals can all add to it.

Pick one thing for a week. Eat slowly and sit down to eat. Try smaller meals instead of one big dinner. Go easy on gum, fizzy drinks and straws. Jot down what you eat and when you feel it.

If gas bothers you, changes suddenly, or comes with pain, constipation, diarrhea or weight loss, skip the experiments and talk to your doctor.

Gentle daily habits, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor.
