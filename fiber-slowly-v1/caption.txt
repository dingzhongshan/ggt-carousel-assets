Fiber is meant to be good for your gut, so why does it sometimes leave you gassy?

The National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases explains that too much fiber at once can cause gas, which can trigger IBS symptoms. Its tip: add fiber a little at a time, by about 2 to 3 grams a day, which may help prevent gas and bloating. It also notes that research suggests soluble fiber (found in beans, fruit and oats) is more helpful for IBS symptoms, and that diet changes can take several weeks to show.

A dietitian or doctor can help you find a pace that suits you.

Gentle daily habits, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor.
