Ever been told to eat more fiber and wondered: which kind?

There are two, and they do different jobs.

Soluble fiber attracts water and turns to gel during digestion, which slows things down. You'll find it in oat bran, barley, beans, lentils, peas, nuts, seeds, and some fruits and vegetables.

Insoluble fiber adds bulk to stool and appears to help food pass more quickly through your stomach and intestines. Think wheat bran, vegetables and whole grains.

NIDDK says adults need 22 to 34 grams of fiber a day, depending on age and sex. Add it a little at a time so your body can adjust, and drink plenty of liquids to help it work.

Gentle daily habits, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor.
