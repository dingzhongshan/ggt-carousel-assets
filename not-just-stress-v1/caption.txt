"It's just stress." "It's all in your head." If you've heard that about your gut, this one is for you.

A new review in The Lancet describes IBS, chronic constipation and functional diarrhoea as disorders of gut-brain interaction. It says they affect about 1 in 5 people worldwide and tend to come and go. The gut-brain signalling is thought to be disrupted, which can change how the gut moves and how sensitive it feels. The causes aren't fully understood yet.

Care usually starts with education, diet and lifestyle advice. Things like weight loss or rectal bleeding are worth telling a doctor about.

Gentle daily habits, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor.
