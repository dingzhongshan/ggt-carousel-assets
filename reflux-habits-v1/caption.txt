That burn behind your breastbone after dinner? Let's talk about it.

NIDDK explains that reflux happens when stomach contents come back up into your esophagus. Once in a while is common. When it keeps coming back and bothers you, that's GERD. Researchers estimate about 20 percent of people in the U.S. have it.

The gentle habits NIDDK lists: eat meals at least 3 hours before you lie down. Raise your head 6 to 8 inches with a foam wedge or extra pillows. And notice whether foods like coffee, chocolate, citrus, mint, spicy or high-fat meals set you off.

See a doctor if over-the-counter medicines and habit changes don't help, or if you have chest pain, trouble swallowing, or signs of bleeding.
